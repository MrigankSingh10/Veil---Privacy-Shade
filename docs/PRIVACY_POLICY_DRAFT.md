# VeilType privacy policy — superseded draft

This earlier draft is retained for reference. The current policy is [privacy-policy.html](privacy-policy.html); keep that page and the in-app policy synchronized.

Last updated: 2 November 2026.

## Information VeilType uses

VeilType provides an optional privacy veil over other apps. When you choose to enable its Android accessibility service, the app observes top-level keyboard window type and bounds, and the foreground app's package name. It uses this information on your device to position the veil and apply your app rules. VeilType does not traverse accessibility nodes or read the text displayed or typed in other apps.

VeilType stores settings on your device, including your selected app rules, schedules, appearance and control position. If you choose a photo, VeilType stores a reference to it and uses Android's document access permission to display it as a veil. The original photo remains with its document provider. Android may retain access grants for selected documents until revoked or the app is uninstalled.

The current app requests no internet permission and does not include accounts, advertising or analytics. It does not send the above information to the developer. Review this statement again before releasing a build with different dependencies or functionality.

## Permissions and your choices

The accessibility service and display-over-other-apps permission are optional and require your action in Android settings. VeilType uses them to detect the keyboard and draw the veil. You can disarm VeilType or revoke access in Android settings. Notification access controls visibility of service controls, subject to Android's behavior. Optional boot access can restore the armed service if you enable that setting.

## Retention and deletion

Preferences and app rules remain on your device until changed or the app's data is cleared or the app is uninstalled. Uninstalling removes the app's local data and its Android document grants; it does not delete the original photo from its provider. A veil is a visual privacy aid and does not prevent screenshots, screen readers or cameras from capturing content.
